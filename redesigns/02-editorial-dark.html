FOXHOLE / SIGNALS / OIL & SHIPPING

Tanker struck in the Strait of Hormuz — and the price of oil has already moved.

A VLCC carrying two million barrels took a hit 40 minutes ago off the Iranian coast. Shipping insurance in the Gulf is up 38% in an hour, spot Brent has added $3.10, and every desk in London is asking the same question: was this a warning, or the first page of something longer?

By Maritime Desk Hormuz — 04:12 GMT 9 sources · Updated 40 min ago BUY  USO / BRENT  ·  87% CONFIDENCE

THE STRAIT, 04:12 GMT — The vessel, identified in multiple AIS and port-agent reports as a very large crude carrier en route to Ras Tanura, reported an explosion abeam the port side. Crew are reported safe; the ship is listing slightly, under tow toward Bandar Abbas, its cargo intact. No claim of responsibility has been made. That silence, traders say, is the loudest signal of all.

Within the hour, war-risk premia for Gulf transits repriced across the market. Tanker brokers quoted a 38% rise in additional premiums; two owners have already diverted laden tonnage via the Cape. Brent futures, thin at the Asian open, gapped to $84.20. Onshore, USO — the United States Oil Fund — is our highest-conviction expression: it tracks WTI directly, holds no shipping name, and does not ask you to pick a winner in the Gulf.

“Every desk is asking whether this was a warning or the first page. The market has answered the easier question first.”

Foxhole rates this BUY, 87% confidence. The thesis is narrow and falsifiable: if a second incident follows within 72 hours, or if the crude sits unclaimed for a full trading day, the premium hardens into a supply shock. If the vessel reaches port and the inquiry comes back “accident,” this desk will say so in print. Until then, the tape, the insurance market and the AIS track are pointing the same way.

What to watch before the New York open: the second VLCC now dark on AIS south of the strait; any NOTAM extension from Tehran; and the Brent term structure — if the front month lifts while the back does not, this is fear. If the whole curve lifts, it is a story.
